## Dangerous prior-art checks

For the current theorem explicitly test:

- [ ] exact same statement under different notation;
- [ ] stronger theorem whose corollary gives our result;
- [ ] same family/classification already known;
- [ ] same structural mechanism already known in an equivalent formulation;
- [ ] bounded computation being mistakenly described as a global theorem;
- [ ] old conference/preprint result superseded by a journal version;
- [ ] later paper by the same authors closing the stated remark/conjecture;
- [ ] recent citing paper that changes open status;
- [ ] equality/extremal case already classified elsewhere;
- [ ] theorem known in a neighboring field under different terminology.

## Citation responsibility

For each source retained in the paper, answer at least one:

- What exact sentence does it support?
- Is it the original source of a definition/problem/theorem?
- Is it the closest direct prior work?
- Is its theorem actually used in the proof?
- Does it establish historical priority?

If none applies, reconsider whether the citation belongs in the manuscript.
