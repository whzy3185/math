# C029 dependence on block inverse and localization methods

Second bounded read-only study, 6 October 2026. The earlier 24-row field report is frozen. This document audits mathematical scope and citation fit; it does not validate the new proof, derive a new theorem, run an experiment, or modify a manuscript.

## Findings that affect positioning

The general machinery is classical: block elimination, inverse-difference identities, inverse decay under spectral separation, weighted matrix norms, geometric summation, bounded-incidence estimates, and quantitative completion of squares. C029's contribution should be evaluated in its explicit graph reduction, certified finite data and the resulting quantitative theorem. It should not be presented as a new general Riccati or localization method.

Three distinctions are essential:

1. A formula for a Schur recurrence does not prove its pivots stay positive
2. An inverse-decay theorem does not certify the positive six-dimensional junction seed or its assembly
3. A bound on the full transfer matrix is not a contraction estimate for C029's selected four-dimensional Schur trajectory

The specific v7 sentence saying that Schur complements and contractive Riccati recurrences are standard is directionally reasonable. Meurant directly supports the block-recurrence part. The inspected sources do not supply the particular contractive-neighborhood theorem used in v7, so citing Meurant alone should not imply that they do.

## Source cards with exact hypotheses

### M1 Meurant 1992

G. Meurant, *A review on the inverse of symmetric tridiagonal and block tridiagonal matrices*, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 13(3), 707–728. DOI [10.1137/0613045](https://doi.org/10.1137/0613045). [Author-hosted journal scan](https://gerard-meurant.fr/blocktridiag_1992.pdf).

Printed p.709 assumes nonsingularity and existence of the Cholesky factorizations; the required principal pivots must be nonsingular. Section 3.1 treats symmetric block tridiagonal matrices. Forward/backward pivots are Schur recurrences; a factorization centered at a chosen block gives inverse columns (Theorem 3.1). Theorems 3.3–3.4 simplify the formulas when off-diagonal blocks are nonsingular (“proper”). Their mechanism is triangular factorization and substitution, not a contraction theorem.

No length-uniform rate follows from factorization alone. Section 4 additionally uses positive definiteness, conditioning, or generalized diagonal dominance. Some displayed PDE bounds carry explicit mesh-dimension prefactors. Separable-case formulas use commuting blocks; C029 must not borrow that commutativity.

Read: standing assumptions, §3.1 and surrounding argument, §3.2 scope, §4.2 through Theorem 4.13. Several scanned formulas have unreliable OCR; no unverified exact formula is transcribed from them. R3 for mechanism/hypotheses; R2 for these OCR-sensitive formula displays.

### M2 Molinari 2013

L. G. Molinari, *Identities and exponential bounds for transfer matrices*, J. Phys. A 46, 254004. DOI [10.1088/1751-8113/46/25/254004](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8113/46/25/254004). [arXiv:1210.8001v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.8001v1), 30 October 2012.

The transfer setup uses square nonsingular coefficient blocks and a doubled state. Theorem 2.3 relates twisted determinants to this full transfer matrix; Proposition 3.2 uses four corner blocks of the open-chain resolvent, at energy outside its spectrum.

Theorem 4.2 independently treats positive-definite block tridiagonal B with spectrum [a,b], a>0: off-diagonal inverse entries obey Cq^|i−j|, with q=(sqrt b−sqrt a)/(sqrt b+sqrt a), C=(sqrt b+sqrt a)²/(2ab); diagonal entries are bounded by 1/a. Polynomial approximation of 1/x and finite propagation prove it. Theorem 4.3 treats invertible nonpositive matrices using singular-value data. Constants are uniform over a family only with uniform spectral/conditioning and block-size bounds.

Proposition 4.4 and Main Theorem 4.1 translate corner decay into expanding/contracting singular directions, with endpoint-dependent constants. Read the relevant setup/results in §§1–4 and §6: R3 for decay/Green-function arguments, R2 for determinant/transfer claims. This is not a proof that C029's T(X) contracts.

### M4 Closest verified recent LAA forward node

P. Boito and Y. Eidelman, *Computation of quasiseparable representations of Green matrices*, LAA 721 (2025), 47–80, DOI [10.1016/j.laa.2024.04.034](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2024.04.034). [arXiv:2308.02701v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.02701v1); journal metadata independently checked against the [author's university record](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/computation-of-quasiseparable-representations-of-green-matrices/).

Theorem 2.1 restates Asplund's rank characterization for inverses of band matrices. Theorems 5.6 and 5.8 assume strong regularity (nonsingular leading principal submatrices) for LU-based lower-/two-sided-band inverse generators. Fixed-bandwidth recursions give compact exact representations and linear-complexity algorithms. Rank structure does not itself supply small generator norms or positivity. The paper explicitly cites Meurant and points forward to decay estimates.

Read: §§1–3, Theorems 5.6/5.8 and preceding factorization, bibliography; R2–R3. Numerical experiments and the complete QR algorithm were not audited. Authors' primary affiliations are Pisa and Tel Aviv, not China. This is a closer methodological match than unrelated recent signed-graph extrema, but remains optional algorithmic context.

### M5 The next decay result in that chain

P. Boito and Y. Eidelman, *Quasiseparable LU decay bounds for inverses of banded matrices*, Numerical Linear Algebra with Applications 32(6) (2025), e70044. DOI [10.1002/nla.70044](https://doi.org/10.1002/nla.70044); [arXiv:2506.16339v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.16339v1).

Theorems 3.1–3.2 assume invertible lower bandwidth r and uniform strong column diagonal dominance: off-diagonal column sum≤mu|a_kk|, 0<mu<1. They bound inverse entries below the diagonal by M gamma^(i−j), gamma=mu^(1/r), M=(1+mu²)/[(1−mu)(1−mu²)min|a_kk|]. Thus uniformity requires fixed bandwidth, mu bounded below 1, and a diagonal lower bound.

The proof preserves dominance under Schur elimination, bounds generator products over r steps, and controls endpoint factors. This is an actual recent alternative to spectral-interval approximation. V7 does not establish this diagonal-dominance hypothesis. Its full block weights P,Q cannot simply be substituted for a diagonal scaling condition.

Read: Theorems 3.1–3.2, Lemma 4.1 through Corollary 4.6 and the proof of Theorem 3.1, §5 scope and references; R3 for LU route, not experiments. Publisher verifies Pisa/Tel Aviv affiliations and publication on 3 November 2025.

### Where the standard ingredients stop

M1 explains the first algebraic elimination route; it does not fill the cap-specific certificate boxes. M2/M3 explain why spectrally separated band interiors have localized inverses; they do not fill the seed, independent-length comparison, or global positivity boxes. ## What finite-seed control adds and what it does not

### Admissible actual trajectory

V7 does not start by assuming an infinite fixed-point solution represents all finite chains. It uses the exact finite entrance X_J and a certified neighborhood. The two-point identity for Phi is obtained by applying the ordinary inverse-difference identity twice, in the correct noncommutative order. The identity itself should not be promoted to new general matrix theory. The substantive local work is establishing that its chosen rational ball is invariant, contains the actual trajectory, and retains positive even and odd pivots at both caps.

### Two distinct orientations

The P inequality controls the two-sided difference in the order-unit norm. The Q inequality controls the propagated rows/columns entering R and W. Source §4 lines 86–105 explicitly tracks the updates R→RT(X) and W→T(X)^T W. A bound in the wrong weighted orientation would leave a genuine gap. Exhibiting convenient rational weights is quantitative certification; weighted contraction as an idea is standard.

### Independent adjacent lengths

The outgoing chain contributes its accumulated G,C,H terms, while the incoming chain contributes the terminal t_(p') term. A closed single-chain seed cannot simply be repeated without restoring its directed terminal pieces. The displayed difference J_(p,p')−S_0 makes those restorations explicit and bounds the two independent indices separately. This is the exact place where a one-chain certificate is converted into a mixed-cell junction estimate.

### Number of cells

Once diagonal and directed errors are bounded, the count-independent assembly estimate uses the elementary inequality 2ab≤a²+b² and one incoming/one outgoing incidence per core. It is not an independent deep localization theorem. The exact combinatorial reduction is essential: loops count twice, both parallel-chain contributions survive at two cells, and each correction must appear once in its correct location.

Earlier Target A already had a minimum-separation IMS error with no explicit count factor inside its stated one-to-three-interface class. This new dependence map therefore supports the more specific claim of explicit arbitrary-count assembly for these certified words, not the invention of count-independent bounds.

### Full-space coercivity

Positive Schur pivots and a retained margin are not, by themselves, a uniform bound in the original Euclidean norm. V7 uses a different input here: the original interior is a compression of cI minus the square of a full periodic adjacency, so intermediate vertices are retained in the two-walk count. It also controls the original coupling by a fixed Gram matrix, rather than a long product of elimination transforms. The final completion-of-squares inequality is standard; the graph-specific gap and coupling identification make its constants uniform.

The proof requires disjoint first/last interior column supports for this Gram identity. The text states j_i≥2 for that step and handles the shorter one-cell cases separately. This qualification is part of the dependence map, even though both long-cell thresholds already satisfy it.
