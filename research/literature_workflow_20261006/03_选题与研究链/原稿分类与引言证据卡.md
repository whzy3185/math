# Fixed-support signing optimization: three focused evidence cards

Read-only research, 6 October 2026. This is an introduction-planning and citation-use report, not replacement manuscript prose or a verification of the project's mathematical claims. The previous 24-row report and method study remain frozen.

## Findings that affect the introduction

1. The original manuscript's classification concerns **when one specified candidate attains the minimum on one prescribed underlying graph**. It does not determine the minimum at the failure orders or list all minimizers. This remains the correct scope even if the word “complete” is used for its truth set.
2. The most direct general problem statement is Belardo–Cioabă–Koolen–Wang, Problem 3.18. General signing existence, classification over all graphs of an order, and evaluating a periodic signing answer three different questions.
3. Finite cyclic-cover spectral decomposition supplies a much closer bridge to the project's finite phase grids than a bare reference to infinite Floquet theory. The exact signed seam and the antiperiodic grid still need the project's own argument.
4. The infinite magnetic spectrum permits translation of a continuous Bloch parameter. Such reparametrization is insufficient to identify two finite holonomy grids. Losing this distinction would erase the mechanism behind the finite antiperiodic improvement.

Math. 1(2), #P2.10; issue year 2018, online publication 7 August 2019. DOI [10.26493/2590-9770.1286.d7b](https://doi.org/10.26493/2590-9770.1286.d7b). Read: [arXiv:1907.04349v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.04349v1), entire §3.5 and relevant definitions.

Problem 3.18 fixes a simple connected unsigned graph and asks for signatures minimizing the **spectral radius**, not merely the index. This is the primary source for that explicit problem formulation; it is a survey rather than the original proof source for all results it recounts. Its discussion separately identifies the one-sided limitation of MSS. Its broader Problem 3.18 is only partially addressed by classifying the success of a prescribed candidate: other minimizers and minima can remain unknown. The USTC and Shandong University of Technology affiliations are on the primary title page.

## Card 1. Bilu–Linial: two-lifts and genuine two-sided signing bounds

**Source.** Yonatan Bilu, Nathan Linial, *Lifts, discrepancy and nearly optimal spectral gap*, Combinatorica 26(5) (2006), 495–519. DOI [10.1007/s00493-006-0029-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00493-006-0029-7). [Institution-hosted published PDF](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~dinuri/courses/20-expanders/projects/bilu-linial.pdf).

**Exact statements.** Lemma 3.1, p.498: a two-lift's adjacency spectrum is the multiset union of the unsigned base spectrum and signed adjacency spectrum. No regularity hypothesis is needed for this identity. Theorem 3.1, §3.3, p.500: every graph of maximum degree d has a signing of spectral radius O(sqrt(d log³d)); this controls both edges. Conjecture 3.1 concerns d-regular graphs and the sharp threshold 2sqrt(d−1).

**Proof mechanism.** Symmetric/antisymmetric fiber vectors give the lift identity. The existence bound uses a local-lemma discrepancy argument followed by Lemma 3.3's dyadic-vector norm recovery.

**Important limitation.** Proposition 3.1's near-tree lower obstruction is asymptotic and conditional. The railway example on p.499 has signing norm sqrt(5)<2sqrt(2), so the universal-cover threshold is not a pointwise lower bound for every support.

**Read depth.** Published pp.498–505, proofs included; start of §3.4 also checked. Later construction/derandomization sections were not fully audited. The earlier arXiv v3 has different subsection numbering; do not transplant those locations into a journal citation.

### Citation use for the project

This is a sound source for why the objective must include the negative spectral edge and for the signed-spectrum/two-lift connection. The old base eigenvalues remain in a lift: a small signed norm alone does not establish that the resulting unsigned lift is Ramanujan. A general existence theorem also supplies neither the twisted candidate's optimality nor a list of exceptional orders. The distinction is about the logical task, not merely the numerical constant.

[arXiv:1304.4132v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.4132v2), Theorems 5.1–5.3 and proof, plus Theorem 5.5 read: arbitrary finite graphs admit a signing with largest eigenvalue at most the universal-cover norm. Applying the theorem separately to opposite signs does not select a common good signing on both edges.

## Card 2. Brunetti–Stanić: actual minimum-radius classification, with a different admissible class

**Source.** Maurizio Brunetti, Zoran Stanić, *Unbalanced signed graphs with extremal spectral radius or index*, Computational and Applied Mathematics 41 (2022), 118. DOI [10.1007/s40314-022-01814-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40314-022-01814-5). [Open-access publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40314-022-01814-5), version of record, 24 March 2022.

**Domain.** All connected, unbalanced, simple signed graphs of order n≥3; the underlying graph varies.

**Theorem 3.5.** It classifies all minimum-**radius** graphs up to switching isomorphism. Minimizers: C₃⁻, C₄⁻; Q₀,₁ at n=5; C₆⁻, Q₁,₁, B₆ at n=6; B₇ at n=7; U₁ at n=8; thereafter Q with balanced pendant lengths, plus Cₙ⁻ when n is even. Values are 2, sqrt(2), sqrt(3) for n=5–8, then 2cos(π/(2ceil(n/2))).

**Separate objective.** Theorem 4.8 gives minimum index 1, attained exactly by the switching class of −Kₙ; that graph's norm is n−1.

**Proof mechanism.** The radius classification combines the cyclotomic induced-subgraph list, unicyclic minimizers, interlacing and finite exceptional cases.

**Read depth.** Definitions, §§2–3, Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.5 proofs, Theorem 4.8 proof and conclusions. Maximum-index proof was only inspected selectively. Graph drawings were not independently reconstructed.

### Citation use for the project

The old introduction may use this as a close example of all-order signed-spectral extremal classification, including finite exceptions. It cannot be described as a theorem about choosing signs on the already fixed C_n(1,2). The minimum over its larger admissible class does not determine the minimum in this prescribed-support subclass. Neither its threshold nor its list resolves the twisted truth set. Its use should occupy a short contextual sentence rather than displace the fixed-graph problem anchor.

### Backward and forward development

The proof explicitly uses McKee–Smyth's cyclotomic classification and Belardo–Brunetti–Ciampella's unicyclic/bicyclic work; these are actual cited inputs. Their original proofs were not all re-audited in this batch.

Conde–Dratman–Grippo, [arXiv:2408.07195v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07195v1), *On the spectral radius of unbalanced signed bipartite graphs*, introduction and conclusion read: the later work addresses the bipartite **maximum**, and explicitly says the minimum is already supplied by the 2022 classification. This is a restricted-class development, not a correction or stronger replacement for Theorem 3.5. A targeted correction/erratum search found no identified reversal of that theorem; this is not an exhaustive correctness clearance.

## Card 3. Finite cyclic lifts: exact all-spectrum sampling and its later extension

**Source.** C. Dalfó, M. A. Fiol, M. Miller, J. Ryan, J. Širáň, *An algebraic approach to lifts of digraphs*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 269 (2019), 68–76. DOI [10.1016/j.dam.2018.10.040](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2018.10.040). [Institutional manuscript](https://oro.open.ac.uk/58309/8/58309.pdf), body dated 25 September 2018; arXiv footer identifies 1612.08855v2. Cite theorem locations to this read version.

**Proposition 5.2.** A finite base digraph on r vertices with voltage assignment in Z_k has lift spectrum equal, with algebraic multiplicities, to all roots of det(λI−B(ω_j)), where ω_j ranges over the k-th roots of unity. The standing digraph setup is strongly connected; loops and multiple arcs are permitted. Undirected graphs are represented using opposite arcs.

**Conclusion type.** A complete spectral identity, not an upper bound or an optimization theorem. General directed matrices need not have real spectra or norm equal to maximum eigenvalue modulus.

**Proof mechanism.** Character-multiplied eigenvectors give the fibers; closed-walk traces/root-of-unity filtering verify multiplicities.

**Read depth.** Setup, polynomial-matrix construction, Proposition 5.2 and its complete proof, and surrounding examples read. No example was recomputed. Its literal statement uses ordinary voltages; the signed seam needs an explicit encoding or direct proof.

### Forward primary confirmation: arbitrary lifts

C. Dalfó, M. A. Fiol, S. Pavlíková, J. Širáň, *Spectra and eigenspaces of arbitrary lifts of graphs*, Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics 54 (2021), 651–672. DOI [10.1007/s10801-021-01049-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10801-021-01049-3). [Publisher OA full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10801-021-01049-3), 23 September 2021.

Theorem 4.1 uses a finite group G and subgroup H of index n, an undirected finite base graph of order k, and inverse voltages on reversed arcs. It gives the kn eigenvalues by concatenating Sp(ρ(B)), each repeated rank(Σ_{h∈H}ρ(h)) times. Regular covers take H trivial; Abelian groups have one-dimensional irreducible representations. The proof constructs all eigenvectors and uses representation orthogonality plus coset-row sums; Corollary 4.2 records eigenspace completeness. Sections 2–4 and the character argument in §6 were read, including the full main proof. This extends the spectral decomposition framework, without adding a signing minimum or a uniform unequal-cell norm estimate. Its reference [5] and §6 explicitly connect to the 2019 Abelian paper.

### Comparison with the project's actual finite-ring assertion

The project's v7 `sections/02_holonomy_bulk.tex`, lines 34–91, identifies all three seam edges, states `u_(i+8m)=alpha*u_i`, and proves the direct sum over `z^m=alpha`. This is stronger documentation for this particular signed matrix than merely citing the general cover formula. Lines 100–112 give constrained optimality among the two classes with the **prescribed labeled** triangle word and attribute the exact formula to the project predecessor.

Three obligations remain project-specific: identify the correct signed fiber; include both discrete phase grids; maximize the relevant absolute eigenvalues over each grid. Evaluating one periodic fiber or one holonomy does not establish unrestricted optimality over all triangle words. The finite-cover papers explain the decomposition step; they do not supply these project conclusions.

## Infinite-periodic bridge and the precise finite-size limitation

Korotyaev–Saburova, *Magnetic Schrödinger operators on periodic discrete graphs*, JFA 272 (2017), 1625–1660, DOI [10.1016/j.jfa.2016.12.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfa.2016.12.015); [arXiv:1512.08207v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.08207v2), 26 November 2016. This round reread Theorem 2.1, Corollary 2.2, §2.2, and the gauge/Fourier proof in §3.

Hypotheses include a connected locally finite Z^d-periodic graph with finite quotient and periodic magnetic/electric potentials. Theorem 2.1 gives Hermitian finite fibers indexed by the continuous torus; equation (2.11) takes their spectral union. Corollary 2.2 removes d flux parameters by translating that torus coordinate. This is a full-spectrum representation of a magnetic Laplacian plus potential, not a fixed-signature minimization theorem. For the degree-four adjacency application, the Laplacian/adjacency convention must be translated explicitly.

**Assessment:** translation preserves a continuous torus but need not preserve the finite allowed sample set. Thus the infinite result does not identify the positive and antiperiodic finite matrices. This is a limitation of the proposed application, not a defect in the cited theorem.

## Introduction argument outline, without manuscript prose

1. Define the underlying graph and objective before discussing methods. Distinguish adjacency signing from squaring a signed cycle.
2. Identify the fixed-support optimization question through Problem 3.18. State exactly which part is answered: the truth set for a specified candidate.
3. Give two-lifts as one concrete reason for the two-sided objective. State the one-sided/bipartite boundary of the interlacing result without suggesting it solves this optimization.
4. If desired, give Brunetti–Stanić as the closest broad classification precedent, explicitly changing the admissible class. Avoid a survey of unrelated maximum-index Turán problems.
5. Separate evaluation of a periodic signing from optimization over all signings. Use finite-cover/Floquet references only for the evaluation mechanism. Keep the exact holonomy grid and inherited project calculation visible.
6. Explain why two proof components are needed for a truth-set theorem: universal finite classification and an analytic strict-competitor tail. Identify the old G6/IMS result before any claimed strengthening.
7. Close the scope precisely: no values of the unknown minima and no complete list of minimizing classes at failure orders. If the manuscript instead remains v7, change the central question accordingly rather than borrowing the old classification title.
