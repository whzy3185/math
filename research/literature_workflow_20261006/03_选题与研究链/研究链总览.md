# C029 field positioning and the relevant research chain

Read-only literature and positioning assessment, 6 October 2026. The manuscript was not edited; no new proof, numerical experiment, formal check, or Git write was performed. This report compares statements and mechanisms, not the correctness of the new mathematical proof.

## Main recommendation

C029 should be positioned as a quantitative, prescribed-construction theorem for signed adjacency matrices on cycle squares: independent unequal cells can be concatenated arbitrarily many times while retaining explicit two-sided norm bounds and a positive margin in the original vertex norm. Its nearest predecessor is the project's separated-G6/IMS estimate, together with its exact period-eight calculation. General signing existence, magnetic/Floquet theory, and block-matrix localization explain the surrounding field and the standard tools. They are not interchangeable novelty comparators.

The sharpest caveat is that the old IMS error was already a function of minimum separation, with no explicit factor equal to the number of interfaces. The old written theorem restricted that number to one, two, or three. The new arbitrary-count theorem is a real change of quantified statement; describing count-independent error itself as unprecedented would conceal the stronger old mechanism. Whether the old local-patch argument extends routinely beyond three interfaces is a separate mathematical evaluation question, not established here.

## Exact object in v7

Source inspected: the local integrated manuscript dated 6 October 2026, especially Sections 1–4 and 6. The main theorem fixes

- support C_N(1,2), a real symmetric signed adjacency matrix, and rho(A)=max|lambda(A)|
- triangle word t=(1,1,-1,1,-1,-1,1,-1), cells w_j=t^j||(1,-1), length h_j=8j+2
- an arbitrary positive number r of cells, independently chosen integers j_i, and either Hamilton-cycle holonomy alpha=±1
- rho(A)^2<7.92 when every h_j≥106; rho(A)^2<7.90537 when every h_j≥202
- full-space margins c_0 I−A²>(1/81000)I and c_1 I−A²>(49/64800000000)I

The one-cell positive-holonomy family also has an all-size bound and a supremum enclosure of width below 10^-6. The unrestricted minimum, optimality of caps or length thresholds, arbitrary defect arrangements, convergence of the one-cell radii, and classification of global minimizers are not claimed. A finite cyclic concatenation is the actual object; an infinite disordered-operator theorem is not stated.

The period-eight antiperiodic formula and its constrained minimizing switching class are inherited results. Their application to the revised Suvagiya conjecture is a current application of that inherited calculation. The even-order witness set is also largely inherited; recomputing certificates for changed finite placements is necessary verification, not ownership of the earlier existence result.

## External evidence matrix

Read-depth codes: R3 = relevant theorem, hypotheses, and associated argument read in a legal primary full text; R2 = relevant exact statement read in primary full text, without full proof audit; R1 = primary abstract/preview or bibliographic record only; R0 = original full text not obtained. ### S1 Switching foundation — recommended

Thomas Zaslavsky, *Signed graphs*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 4 (1982), 47–74. DOI [10.1016/0166-218X(82)90033-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(82)90033-6). [Primary paper scan](https://davinci.fmph.uniba.sk/~matok1/diplomovka/sources/Zaslavsky-former.pdf), printed pp.51–52; R3.

Lemma 3.1 gives a unique representative of each switching class agreeing with any prescribed signing on a maximal forest. Proposition 3.2 identifies classes by their balanced cycles. This is the exact foundation for Hamilton-path normalization and retained cycle products. C029's two classes after all triangle signs are fixed specialize the general principle to a particular cycle basis. Important numbering: the original calls 3.2 a Proposition, not a Theorem. No need to claim new gauge theory.

### S2 Fixed-support two-sided signing — recommended concise background

Yonatan Bilu and Nathan Linial, *Lifts, discrepancy and nearly optimal spectral gap*, Combinatorica 26 (2006), 495–519. DOI [10.1007/s00493-006-0029-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00493-006-0029-7). [arXiv:math/0312022v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0312022v3), revised 8 April 2004 under the earlier title *Constructing expander graphs by 2-lifts and discrepancy vs. spectral gap*, §§3.1–3.2; R3 for the lift identity, local obstruction, and example; R2 for general existence.

The new spectrum of a 2-lift is the signed adjacency spectrum. Every maximum-degree-d graph admits norm O(sqrt(d log³d)). Their railway example has norm sqrt5 below the cubic universal-cover threshold 2sqrt2, while sufficiently large tree neighborhoods obstruct such improvement. This makes local cycle geometry a genuine motivation for C029. It does not identify a cycle-square minimum. Low signed new eigenvalues alone do not remove the old eigenvalues of a lift, so C029 should not be advertised as a new expander construction.

### S3 Interlacing existence — background only

Adam W. Marcus, Daniel A. Spielman and Nikhil Srivastava, *Interlacing families I: Bipartite Ramanujan graphs of all degrees*, Annals of Mathematics 182 (2015), 307–325. DOI [10.4007/annals.2015.182.1.7](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2015.182.1.7). [arXiv:1304.4132v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.4132v2), Theorems 5.2–5.5 and §2.2; R3 for the stated implication, not the entire stability proof.

Theorem 5.3 supplies a signing whose largest eigenvalue is bounded by the universal-cover radius; two-sided control follows in the bipartite case. Cycle squares contain triangles, and a fixed prescribed signing is not selected by this theorem. It therefore supplies context, not the C029 estimates. The roots of the expected characteristic polynomial are not the same object as an average of signed moments or the characteristic polynomial of A².

### S4 A real forward development of S3 — optional

Mohan Ravichandran and Nikhil Srivastava, *Asymptotically optimal multi-paving*, IMRN 2021(14), 10908–10940; first published online 2019. DOI [10.1093/imrn/rnz111](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnz111). [arXiv:1706.03737v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03737v2), §7.2, p.21 of preprint; R3 for that short application.

The mixed determinantal polynomials chi[A_sigma,−A_sigma] form an interlacing family. A mixed-root bound is converted to a two-sided signing bound 2sqrt(2(d−1)). This is an actual development in simultaneous control, unlike simply citing another Ramanujan construction. The bound is existential over signings, not a prescribed-word cap, and is quantitatively weak at degree four.

### S5 Recent China-affiliated two-sided signing work — optional current context

Fangfang Lin and Hong Zhou, *Improved Bounds for the Bilu–Linial Conjecture via Spectral Recovery from Mixed Determinantal Polynomials*, [arXiv:2609.15715v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15715v1), 14 September 2026. DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2609.15715](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.15715). Both affiliations are Fuzhou University. R2: Theorems 1.2, 1.5, 1.6, Corollary 1.4 and technical overview.

For a d-regular finite graph and a signing satisfying r_A²≤2(d−1), Theorem 1.2 gives rho(A)<((3+sqrt5)/2)sqrt(d−1); the prior interlacing theorem supplies such a signing. Triangle-free/girth refinements have additional hypotheses absent in C_N(1,2). Theorem 1.6 limits the conversion method using a K8 signing. For d=4 the general bound exceeds 4, so this is not a useful competitor to C029's cap. It is a recent preprint, not a journal-established premise for C029.

### S6 Another recent China-affiliated route — hold chronology

Zejun Huang, *A sqrt2-Approximation to the Bilu–Linial Conjecture*, [arXiv:2609.17551v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17551v1). DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2609.17551](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.17551). Shenzhen University affiliation. R3: complete short HTML mathematical body read.

Theorem 1.2 treats finite simple graphs of maximum degree d≥3 and gives rho≤4sqrt(ceil(d/2)−1) by a balanced orientation and an auxiliary bipartite signing. At d=4 this is 4. It is broad background only. The fetched primary page and HTML both say July 2026 while the identifier has prefix 2609; the chronology remains flagged for verification. Do not use a guessed submission date or portray this as C029's nearest prior theorem.

### S7 Random near-Ramanujan signings — screened out of the main story

Sidhanth Mohanty, Ryan O'Donnell and Pedro Paredes, *Explicit near-Ramanujan graphs of every degree*. [arXiv:1909.06988v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.06988v3), revised 27 November 2022, Theorem 1.2; SIAM Journal on Computing, DOI [10.1137/20M1342112](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1342112). R2 for theorem and definition.

The signing theorem assumes every radius-r neighborhood has at most one cycle, with r much larger than (log log n)², and concludes a high-probability two-sided bound 2sqrt(d−1)+o(1). Cycle squares have overlapping short cycles; the hypothesis does not fit. No probabilistic conclusion for C029 follows. Include only if explaining why a cited near-Ramanujan theorem is inapplicable; it need not occupy an introduction paragraph.

### S8 Unit-gain formulation — useful but not essential if S1 suffices

Nathan Reff, *Spectral properties of complex unit gain graphs*, LAA 436 (2012), 3165–3176. DOI [10.1016/j.laa.2011.10.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2011.10.021). [arXiv:1110.4554v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.4554v2), revised 15 November 2011, Lemma 2.2, Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.3; R3.

For reverse-oriented reciprocal unit gains, switching is diagonal unitary conjugation and preserves adjacency spectrum. The maximum-degree norm bound is rho≤Delta. This supports complex phase fibers and the signed special case; it neither determines the minimizing holonomy nor supplies a uniform defect bound. Do not import the paper's Laplacian results without converting the operator.

### S9 Periodic magnetic fibers — recommended method background

Evgeny Korotyaev and Natalia Saburova, *Magnetic Schrödinger operators on periodic discrete graphs*, JFA 272 (2017), 1625–1660. DOI [10.1016/j.jfa.2016.12.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfa.2016.12.015). [arXiv:1512.08207v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.08207v2), Theorem 2.1, remarks, §3.1; R2–R3.

Periodic locally finite graphs with finite quotient and periodic magnetic/electric potentials admit explicit flux-based direct-integral fibers. Gauges with equal cycle fluxes are unitarily equivalent. On regular supports, magnetic Laplacian and adjacency differ by a scalar and sign, but their named spectral quantities must still be distinguished. This explains periodic bulk analysis. It does not diagonalize arbitrary unequal concatenations into one fixed small fiber, and it does not automatically establish C029's caps.

### S10 Recent LAA node with genuine technical relevance — optional concise addition

Evgeny Korotyaev and Natalia Saburova, *Trace formulas for magnetic Schrödinger operators on periodic graphs and their applications*, LAA 676 (2023), 395–440. DOI [10.1016/j.laa.2023.07.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2023.07.025). [arXiv:2206.09663v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09663v2), 8 August 2023; journal issue 1 November 2023. R3: Theorem 3.2 and proof; R2: periodic setup.

The magnetic-adjacency fiber trace of each power is a finite Fourier sum over quotient closed walks, weighted by flux and displacement; averaging over quasimomentum retains zero-displacement walks. This is close to C029's matrix-valued periodic bulk and signed walk expansion. It is not a theorem controlling unequal cells or the maximum of every band. Its recent-issue status must not be confused with the earlier preprint date. Cite only if this relationship is explained, rather than merely because it is in LAA.

### S11 Recent character and endpoint theorem — useful context, limited role

Ye Luo and Arindam Roy, *Homological spectral graph theory and weighted cycle counting*, [arXiv:2403.01550v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01550v4), source version cited in v7 as 4 August 2026; DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2403.01550](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.01550). Luo's affiliation is Xiamen University. R2: Theorem 3.6 and Theorem 3.13, including preceding notation.

Theorem 3.6 treats a connected finite graph, positive directed weights and its character torus; symmetric weights give Hermitian vertex operators. For nowhere-zero real symmetric periodic weights, Theorem 3.13 treats the affine sign-shifted Bloch torus and characterizes attainment of the unsigned outer edges by membership of the trivial/canonical characters. These are outer-edge maxima relative to positive weights, not minimization over all real signings. C029's fixed-word finite phase grid and nonzero local frustration still need their own calculation.

### S12 Block inverse recurrences — recommended close matrix reference

Gérard Meurant, *A review on the inverse of symmetric tridiagonal and block tridiagonal matrices*, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 13 (1992), 707–728. DOI [10.1137/0613045](https://doi.org/10.1137/0613045). [Author-hosted journal PDF](https://gerard-meurant.fr/blocktridiag_1992.pdf), §3.1, pp.714–716; R3 for the block-factorization argument, R2 for inverse formulas.

For symmetric block tridiagonal systems with the required invertible block pivots, forward/backward Schur recurrences yield inverse columns. Additional simplified factorization formulas require nonsingular off-diagonal blocks (“proper” matrices). This is a direct standard-method ancestor for length-elimination and endpoint responses. C029's extra content is its certified invariant neighborhood, precise fixed seed and assembly bounds; merely writing a matrix Riccati recurrence is not a novelty claim.

### S13 Inverse decay foundation — original not read

Stephen Demko, William F. Moss and Philip W. Smith, *Decay rates for inverses of band matrices*, Mathematics of Computation 43 (1984), 491–499. DOI [10.1090/S0025-5718-1984-0758197-9](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1984-0758197-9). Original read depth R0: official PDF web retrieval failed; a read-only download attempt was cancelled by approval review and was not retried.

The exact positive-definite block specialization was read with proof in S14, Theorem 4.2, and the banded formulation in S15 §3. It requires a spectral interval [a,b] with a>0; constants depend on a,b and bandwidth. This is the provenance of exponential localization, not a basis for pretending positivity has already been proved for a whole C029 ring.

### S14 Block Green functions and transfer bounds — recommended close matrix reference

Luca Guido Molinari, *Identities and exponential bounds for transfer matrices*, J. Phys. A 46 (2013), 254004. DOI [10.1088/1751-8113/46/25/254004](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8113/46/25/254004). [arXiv:1210.8001v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.8001v1), Theorem 2.3, §3, Theorems 4.2–4.3; R3 for §4 inverse-decay argument, R2 for transfer duality.

For positive definite block tridiagonal A with spectrum in [a,b], inverse entries in block (i,j) decay as Cq^|i−j|, q=(sqrt b−sqrt a)/(sqrt b+sqrt a); invertible nonpositive cases use singular-value data. Boundary-twisted determinants are related to transfer matrices under the invertible coupling hypotheses of that setup. This is the closest external structural analogy found. It does not certify C029's seed positivity, exact length thresholds, or a count-uniform assembled Schur complement.

### S15 Forward decay development — optional, not needed alongside every older reference

Michele Benzi and Valeria Simoncini, *Decay bounds for functions of Hermitian matrices with banded or Kronecker structure*, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 36 (2015), 1263–1282. DOI [10.1137/151006159](https://doi.org/10.1137/151006159). [Author-hosted preprint](https://www.dm.unibo.it/~simoncin/kron_revised2.pdf), §3 and Theorem 4.6 with derivation; R3 for these sections.

A banded Hermitian positive-definite matrix and an appropriate Cauchy–Stieltjes function admit decay bounds obtained by integrating resolvent bounds. The paper explicitly traces inverse decay to S13 and cites S12 and S14. Dimension-independent localization requires uniform spectral data; it is not guaranteed merely by sparsity. C029 proves the data and local gluing estimates in its particular family. The wider matrix-function and Kronecker applications are not needed in its introduction.

### S16 A current bibliography item in LAA — adjacent comparator

M. Rajesh Kannan and Shivaram Pragada, *Signed spectral Turán type theorems*, LAA 663 (2023), 62–79. DOI [10.1016/j.laa.2023.01.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2023.01.002). [arXiv:2204.09870v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.09870v3), Theorem 3.3 and proof; R3.

For a signed graph with m edges, frustration index epsilon and balanced clique number omega_b, lambda_max²≤2(m−epsilon)(1−1/omega_b). This is pointwise but uses coarse global combinatorial parameters. Applying such results separately to a signing and its negation can bound both spectral edges; therefore “one-sided” alone is not a decisive exclusion. The missing item is C029's sharp, prescribed-word, dimension-independent cap. This 2023 issue is outside a strict October-2023-to-October-2026 window and should not be relabeled as a recent-three-year paper.

### S17 Recent China-affiliated moment identity — optional, not proof support

Lixiang Chen, Edwin R. van Dam and Changjiang Bu, *Spectra of power hypergraphs and signed graphs via parity-closed walks*, JCTA 207 (2024), 105909. DOI [10.1016/j.jcta.2024.105909](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2024.105909). [arXiv:2302.10496v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.10496v1), Theorem 3.1; R3 for the averaging identity and local argument. Chen/Bu have Harbin Engineering University affiliations in the source.

For any graph, the number of length-d closed walks using each edge evenly equals the mean dth signed spectral moment over all signings. This is an exact averaging theorem. It is not a pointwise lower bound for every signing and does not select C029's words. Its hypergraph multiplicity conclusions need not be described unless the manuscript actually uses them.

### S18 Limit points — adjacent, not the main chain

Francesco Belardo and Maurizio Brunetti, *Limit points for the spectral radii of signed graphs*, Discrete Mathematics 347(2) (2024), 113745. DOI [10.1016/j.disc.2023.113745](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2023.113745). R1–R2: primary indexed full-text passages of Theorem 1.3 and its setting read; direct publisher/institutional PDF opening failed, so Lemma 3.8 was not independently read here.

Theorem 1.3 describes additional signed-graph limit points below the Hoffman threshold sqrt(2+sqrt5), beyond those obtained from balanced graphs. Above that threshold, general limit-point existence is already supplied by tree sequences. C029's finite supremum enclosure neither proves convergence nor contributes a new unrestricted limit point. Keep this comparison only if needed to prevent a misleading interpretation of the one-cell statement.
