# A finite absorber principle for odd vertical resonances

This note turns the two-seam-defect identity into a reusable theorem.  Its main point is that an **infinite vertical family** can be certified by two fixed finite matrices together with finitely many small chord-cycle lengths.

Throughout

\[
N=ks,
\qquad k\ge3\text{ odd},
\qquad s\ge5\text{ odd},
\]

and we use the Hamilton-seam/chord-alternating signing from
`ODD_RESONANCE_SEAM_DEFECT_FRAMEWORK.md`.  After multiplication-by-two reindexing,

\[
K:=8I-A^2=L_\Sigma+E_-+E_+,
\tag{1}
\]

where `L_Sigma` is a signed Laplacian on the underlying graph `C_N(1,s)`.

Put

\[
a=\frac{s+1}{2},
\qquad
b=\frac{s-1}{2},
\qquad
K_0=\frac{k-1}{2}.
\]

The two exceptional endpoint sets are

\[
D_-:=\{0,-a\},
\qquad
D_+:=\{K_0s,K_0s+b\}
\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z_N.
\tag{2}
\]

(The second description is identical to the endpoints
`{(N-s)/2,(N-1)/2}`.)

## Lemma 1 (exact seam separation)

In the ordinary graph metric of `C_N(1,s)`,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{dist}(D_-,D_+)=K_0=\frac{k-1}{2}.}
\tag{3}
\]

### Proof

For the Cayley graph generated by `+-1,+-s`, the distance from `0` to a residue `d` is

\[
\min_{m\in\mathbb Z}\bigl(|m|+|d-ms|\bigr),
\tag{4}
\]

where changing `m` by a multiple of `k` incorporates the possible wrap by `N=ks`.

The four differences from `D_-` to `D_+` are represented by

\[
K_0s,
\qquad
K_0s+b,
\qquad
K_0s+a,
\qquad
(K_0+1)s.
\]

Their distances are respectively

\[
K_0,
\qquad
K_0+b,
\qquad
K_0+a,
\qquad
K_0+1.
\tag{5}
\]

To see that the first value cannot be shortened, any representation
`K_0s=m s+r` with `r` unit steps has length `|m|+|r|`; modulo `ks`, choose `m` in the nearest congruence class to `K_0`.  Since `K_0=(k-1)/2` is the distance from `0` to the antipodal-nearest row on the odd `k`-cycle, `|m|>=K_0` when `r=0`, while introducing `r!=0` only increases the length.  The same one-dimensional minimization in (4) gives the remaining three values in (5).  Thus the minimum is `K_0`. `square`

## 2. Local absorber matrices

For an exceptional endpoint set `D` and radius `r`, let

\[
B_r(D)
\]

be its radius-`r` vertex ball in the base graph `C_N(1,s)`, and let

\[
F_r(D)
\]

be the set of base edges with both endpoints in that ball.

Define the two local quadratic forms

\[
Q_-(s,r_-)
:=
\sum_{ij\in F_{r_-}(D_-)}
(x_i-\sigma_{ij}x_j)^2
-4x_0x_{-a},
\tag{6}
\]

and

\[
Q_+(s,r_+)
:=
\sum_{ij\in F_{r_+}(D_+)}
(x_i-\sigma_{ij}x_j)^2
+4x_{K_0s}x_{K_0s+b}.
\tag{7}
\]

When the two seam neighborhoods are separated, the signed patterns of (6)--(7) depend only on `(s,r_-)` and `(s,r_+)`, not on `k`, up to translation and relabelling.  This follows directly from the explicit step-one and step-`s` sign formulas in the two-seam-defect theorem: inside either ball no edge can encounter the other seam region.

## Theorem 2 (finite absorber principle)

Assume that for some nonnegative integers `r_-,r_+` the two fixed local matrices representing (6)--(7) are positive definite.  Then every odd chord-cycle length satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\frac{k-1}{2}>r_-+r_+
}
\tag{8}
\]

obeys

\[
\boxed{m(ks,s)<\sqrt8.}
\tag{9}
\]

Equivalently, it is enough that

\[
k\ge 2(r_-+r_+)+3
\]

for odd `k`.

### Proof

By Lemma 1, (8) implies that the two vertex balls

\[
B_{r_-}(D_-),
\qquad
B_{r_+}(D_+)
\]

are disjoint.  Hence their induced base-edge sets are disjoint as well.

Using the quadratic-form version of (1), split all base edges into the two absorber sets and the unallocated remainder.  Then exactly

\[
x^TKx
=
Q_-(s,r_-)+Q_+(s,r_+)
+
\sum_{ij\notin F_{r_-}(D_-)\cup F_{r_+}(D_+)}
(x_i-\sigma_{ij}x_j)^2.
\tag{10}
\]

The first two terms are positive definite on their local coordinate sets by hypothesis; every remaining term is nonnegative.  Thus the right-hand side is nonnegative.

If equality held for a nonzero `x`, positive definiteness of the two local forms would force all coordinates in both absorber balls to vanish.  Every base edge crossing from either ball to its complement is unallocated and therefore appears in the last sum of (10); its square then forces the adjacent outside coordinate to vanish.  Since `C_N(1,s)` is connected, this zero value propagates through the whole base graph.  Hence equality implies `x=0`, so

\[
K=8I-A^2\succ0.
\]

This gives `rho(A)<sqrt(8)` and proves (9). `square`

## Corollary 2.1 (finite certification of an entire vertical family)

Fix an odd step `s>=5`.  If one finds radii `r_-,r_+` for which the two local absorber matrices are positive definite, then only the finitely many odd values

\[
3\le k<2(r_-+r_+)+3
\]

remain to be checked separately.  Even `k` are already sub-`sqrt(8)` by the all-even-order theorem.

Thus an infinite vertical family reduces to:

1. two finite exact positive-definiteness certificates depending only on `s`;
2. finitely many small odd chord-cycle lengths;
3. the already known global obstruction at `k=3` when `s>=7` is odd.

This is the common proof mechanism behind the vertical results for steps `5,7,9,11,13` and the near-classifications for steps `15,17`.

## 3. Audited absorber radii currently available

The exact certificates already in this branch give, for example,

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
s&r_-&r_+&\text{safe odd }k\text{ from Theorem 2}\\ \hline
5&1&2&k\ge9,\\
7&2&2&k\ge11,\\
9&3&3&k\ge15,\\
11&4&4&k\ge19,\\
13&4&4&k\ge19,\\
15&5&5&k\ge23,\\
17&5&5&k\ge23.
\end{array}
\tag{11}
\]

Several individual vertical proofs improve these safe separation thresholds because their **edge sets** become disjoint before the full vertex balls do; the table is intentionally a uniform sufficient bound, not a claim of optimality.

The conceptual gain is that no new large-`k` spectral calculation is needed once the two local matrices are certified.
