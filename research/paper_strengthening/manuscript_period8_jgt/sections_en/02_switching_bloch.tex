\section{Switching coordinates and periodic fibers}
\label{sec:gauge}

The optimization in \eqref{eq:fixed-minimum} is stated in terms of edge
signs, but edge signs are not the invariant data.  We first pass to local
cycle signs and retain the one global sign that cannot be removed on a finite
Hamilton cycle.  Periodicity of the resulting word then supplies the finite
Bloch decomposition used throughout the paper.

\subsection{Signed cycle squares and Hamilton gauge}

Write $s_i$ for the sign of the step-one edge $\{i,i+1\}$ and $t_i$ for the
sign of the step-two edge $\{i,i+2\}$, with indices modulo $n$.  A switching
function $\varepsilon: \mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\to\{\pm1\}$ sends
\[
 s_i\longmapsto \varepsilon_i s_i\varepsilon_{i+1},
 \qquad
 t_i\longmapsto \varepsilon_i t_i\varepsilon_{i+2}.
\]
If $D_\varepsilon=\diag(\varepsilon_i)$, the signed adjacency matrices are
related by $A\mapsto D_\varepsilon A D_\varepsilon$.  Thus switching is an
orthogonal similarity.

The sign of the triangle $(i,i+1,i+2)$ is
\begin{equation}
 \tau_i=s_i s_{i+1}t_i,
 \label{eq:triangle-flux}
\end{equation}
and the sign of the step-one Hamilton cycle is
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}s_i.
 \label{eq:ham-holonomy}
\end{equation}
Both are switching invariant.  Conversely, they determine the switching
class.  Indeed, choose $\varepsilon_0=1$ and then recursively choose
$\varepsilon_{i+1}=\varepsilon_i s_i$ along the cut-open Hamilton path.  All
local step-one signs become positive, the step-two sign based at $i$ becomes
$\tau_i$, and the mismatch after one circuit is $\alpha$.

This construction is best stated on the lift.  A vector obeys
\begin{equation}
 x_{i+n}=\alpha x_i,
 \label{eq:finite-holonomy-boundary}
\end{equation}
and the Hamilton-gauge operator is
\begin{equation}
 (A_\tau x)_i=x_{i-1}+x_{i+1}
   +\tau_{i-2}x_{i-2}+\tau_i x_{i+2}.
 \label{eq:hamilton-operator}
\end{equation}
Thus ``positive step-one gauge'' is a local normalization on the cut-open
lift; negative holonomy is carried by the boundary condition, not by a claim
that the product of positive finite-cycle edges is negative.

\subsection{Symmetries and primitive period}
\label{sec:symmetries}

The local-square word
\begin{equation}
 Q_i=\tau_i\tau_{i+1}
 \label{eq:q-word}
\end{equation}
is invariant under replacing $\tau$ by $-\tau$.  A $p$-periodic $Q$-word has
a $p$-periodic lift precisely when $\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}Q_i=1$; after $\tau_0$
is chosen, the recurrence $\tau_{i+1}=Q_i\tau_i$ gives the two lifts $\tau$
and $-\tau$.

For later orbit reductions we record the spectral effect of this choice.  Let
$\mathcal B_z^{(p)}$ be the space of sequences with $x_{i+p}=zx_i$, where
$|z|=1$, and let $H_\tau^{(p)}(z)$ be the restriction of
\eqref{eq:hamilton-operator}.  Set
\begin{equation}
 R_p(\tau)=\max_{|z|=1}\rho(H_\tau^{(p)}(z))^2.
 \label{eq:bloch-edge}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Lift and dihedral invariance]
\label{prop:invariances}
The quantity $R_p$ has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $R_p(-\tau)=R_p(\tau)$.
 \item A cyclic translate of $\tau$ has the same fiber spectrum at each
       phase $z$.
 \item If $\tau^\vee_i=\tau_{-i-2}$, then
       $\Spec H_{\tau^\vee}^{(p)}(z^{-1})=
        \Spec H_\tau^{(p)}(z)$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently $R_p$ is constant on the dihedral orbits of legal $Q$-words and
is independent of the choice of lift.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $(Dx)_i=(-1)^i x_i$.  Displacement-one terms change sign under conjugation
by $D$, while displacement-two terms do not, and hence
\[
 D A_\tau D=-A_{-\tau}.
\]
The map $D$ sends $\mathcal B_z^{(p)}$ to
$\mathcal B_{(-1)^p z}^{(p)}$.  Negating an operator leaves squared
eigenvalues unchanged, and $z\mapsto(-1)^pz$ permutes the unit circle.  This
proves the first assertion.

For the other two, use $(S_kx)_i=x_{i+k}$ and $(\mathcal Rx)_i=x_{-i}$.
Substitution in \eqref{eq:hamilton-operator} gives
\[
 A_{\operatorname{rot}_k\tau}S_k=S_kA_\tau,
 \qquad
 A_{\tau^\vee}\mathcal R=\mathcal RA_\tau.
\]
The shift $S_k$ preserves $\mathcal B_z^{(p)}$, whereas $\mathcal R$ maps it
to $\mathcal B_{z^{-1}}^{(p)}$.  Both maps are unitary.  The induced actions on
$Q$ may change the chosen lift only by a global minus sign, which has already
been handled.
\end{proof}

A displayed cell need not be primitive.  If $\tau$ has least period $q$ but
is displayed in a cell of length $p=kq$, translation by $q$ further
diagonalizes the $p$-fiber and gives the zone-folding identity
\begin{equation}
 \Spec H_\tau^{(p)}(z)
  =\bigcup_{w^k=z}\Spec H_\tau^{(q)}(w),
 \label{eq:zone-folding}
\end{equation}
with multiplicities.  Taking the union over $|z|=1$ proves that cell
repetition does not change $R_p$.  We therefore use \emph{primitive period}
for the least period of $\tau$ and reserve \emph{displayed period} for the
chosen cell size.

\subsection{Finite Bloch decomposition}
\label{sec:finite-bloch}

Suppose now that $\tau$ has displayed period $p$ and $n=pL$.  Group the
coordinates into $L$ cells,
\[
 X_r=(x_{rp},x_{rp+1},\ldots,x_{rp+p-1})^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad 0\leq r<L.
\]
The cell shift is unitary and its $L$ eigenvalues are precisely the phases
satisfying $z^L=\alpha$.  Its eigenspace with eigenvalue $z$ consists of
$X_r=z^rX_0$.  Since the Hamilton-gauge operator commutes with the cell shift,
these eigenspaces are invariant and its restriction is the $p\times p$
Hermitian matrix $H_\tau^{(p)}(z)$.

\begin{proposition}[Finite Bloch sum]
\label{prop:finite-bloch}
For every $L\geq1$ and $\alpha\in\{\pm1\}$,
\begin{equation}
 A_{pL,\alpha}(\tau)
 \simeq\bigoplus_{z^L=\alpha}H_\tau^{(p)}(z).
 \label{eq:finite-bloch-sum}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \rho(A_{pL,\alpha}(\tau))
 =\max_{z^L=\alpha}\rho(H_\tau^{(p)}(z)).
 \label{eq:finite-radius-max}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The eigenspaces of the unitary cell shift are mutually orthogonal, each has
dimension $p$, and there are $L$ of them.  Their dimensions sum to $pL$, so
the restrictions give the full matrix with no missing multiplicities.  For
$|z|=1$, every boundary-crossing entry is paired with its complex conjugate;
hence each fiber is Hermitian.  The spectral-radius identity follows from the
orthogonal direct sum.
\end{proof}

The decomposition reduces a periodic signing to a family of finite Hermitian
matrices.  The next question is structural: which flux patterns force these
fiber spectra to be symmetric about zero?  The half-cell criterion answering
this question is independent of period eight.
