\section{First occurrence and rigidity}
\label{sec:first-rigidity}

The preceding section gives a distinguished period-eight phase, but an exact
solution of one word does not explain the period.  We now ask two successive
questions.  Can a shorter primitive word have squared Bloch edge below $8$?
If not, how many low-edge phases occur at the first feasible period?  A local
square identity and three trace moments reduce both questions to a small
number of exact configurations.

\subsection{Local squares and moment reduction}

Squaring \eqref{eq:hamilton-operator} makes the role of $Q$ visible.  Direct
substitution gives
\begin{align}
(A_\tau^2x)_i={}&4x_i+x_{i-2}+x_{i+2}
 +\tau_{i-4}\tau_{i-2}x_{i-4}+\tau_i\tau_{i+2}x_{i+4}\notag\\
&+\tau_{i-3}(1+Q_{i-3})x_{i-3}
 +\tau_{i-2}(1+Q_{i-2})x_{i-1}\notag\\
&+\tau_{i-1}(1+Q_{i-1})x_{i+1}
 +\tau_i(1+Q_i)x_{i+3}.
\label{eq:local-square-row}
\end{align}
Thus $Q_i=-1$ cancels the corresponding odd-displacement coupling, while
$Q_i=1$ activates a term of absolute value two.  We call a positive $Q$-site
a \emph{defect}.

For a $p$-periodic word, define the phase-averaged even moments
\begin{equation}
 M_k(\tau)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
  \tr\!\left(H_\tau^{(p)}(e^{\mathrm i\theta})^{2k}\right)
  \,d\theta.
 \label{eq:phase-averaged-moments}
\end{equation}
Let $d$ be the number of positive entries of $Q$, and let
\begin{equation}
 a=\#\{i:Q_i=Q_{i+1}=1\},\qquad
 b=\#\{i:Q_i=Q_{i+2}=1\},
 \label{eq:defect-statistics}
\end{equation}
with cyclic indices.  Taking the diagonal of
\eqref{eq:local-square-row}, then the squared row norm, gives the first two
moments.  For the third, expand
$\sum_{i,j,k}(A_\tau^2)_{ij}(A_\tau^2)_{jk}(A_\tau^2)_{ki}$ using the same
nine displacements; translation-equivalent terms group according to one
positive site, an adjacent positive pair, or a distance-two positive pair.
The result is
\begin{equation}
 M_1=4p,\qquad
 M_2=20p+16d,\qquad
 M_3=118p+168d+96a+48b.
 \label{eq:first-three-moments}
\end{equation}
For $M_2$, $(1+Q_i)^2=2(1+Q_i)$ and every $Q_i$ occurs in four
odd-displacement row entries, producing $16d$.  The coefficients in $M_3$ are
the grouped weights of the three local types just listed.

If $R_p(\tau)\leq8$, every squared fiber eigenvalue $y$ satisfies
$0\leq y\leq8$.  Hence $y^{k+1}\leq8y^k$, and averaging gives
$M_{k+1}\leq8M_k$.  Applying this for $k=1,2$ yields
\begin{equation}
 16d-12p\leq0,\qquad
 40d+96a+48b-42p\leq0.
 \label{eq:moment-excess-inequalities}
\end{equation}
These inequalities perform the structural reduction before any finite case is
checked.

\subsection{Periods below eight}

A legal $Q$-word satisfies $\prod_iQ_i=1$.  Combining legality,
\eqref{eq:moment-excess-inequalities}, and the dihedral invariance of
Proposition~\ref{prop:invariances} leaves the following representatives for
$p<8$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|l}
p&\text{representatives not excluded by the moments}\\ \hline
1&\text{none}\\
2&--\\
3&--+\\
4&----\\
5&----+\\
6&------,\;----++,\;---+-+,\;--+--+\\
7&------+ ,\;---+-++,\;--+--++,\;--+-+-+
\end{array}
\label{eq:short-survivors}
\end{equation}
The all-negative words have even period; their lifts alternate and have
squared edge exactly $8$.  Every other row is closed by the next exact lemma.

Choose the lift with $\tau_0=1$, and put
\[
 C_\tau(z)=8I-H_\tau^{(p)}(z)^2.
\]
A vector $v$ with $v^*C_\tau(z)v<0$ proves that the corresponding squared
fiber has an eigenvalue above $8$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact closure of the short-period survivors]
\label{lem:short-certificates}
For the $p=3$ representative in \eqref{eq:short-survivors}, at
$z=e^{\pi\mathrm i/3}$,
\[
 \det C_\tau(z)=-1.
\]
For every remaining nonbalanced representative, Table~\ref{tab:certificates}
gives a vector $v$ and a phase $z$ for which $v^*C_\tau(z)v<0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each row is a direct multiplication in a matrix of order at most seven.  The
displayed value is negative, so $C_\tau(z)$ is not positive semidefinite and
$H_\tau^{(p)}(z)^2$ has an eigenvalue larger than $8$.  For $p=3$, the matrix
$C_\tau(e^{\pi\mathrm i/3})$ is Hermitian and has negative determinant, so it
also has a negative eigenvalue.  These checks exhaust the representatives in
\eqref{eq:short-survivors}; no numerical spectral approximation is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Smallest primitive period]
\label{thm:minimal-period}
No legal word of primitive period below eight has squared Bloch edge below
$8$.  The word $\tau_*$ has primitive period eight and edge
$\etaedge<8$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The displayed-period claim follows from
\eqref{eq:short-survivors}, Lemma~\ref{lem:short-certificates}, and the exact
edge of the balanced alternating word.  Equation \eqref{eq:zone-folding}
shows that repeating a cell does not change its Bloch edge.  Finally,
$\tau_{i+4}=-\tau_i$ excludes period four, and its first four signs exclude
period one or two; hence $\tau_*$ is primitive of period eight.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rigidity at period eight}

At the first feasible period, the same reduction is even sharper.  Applying
legality and \eqref{eq:moment-excess-inequalities} with $p=8$ leaves, up to
dihedral symmetry, the balanced word and four two-defect words.  One has
antipodal defects and is $Q_*$.  The other three have cyclic defect separations
$1,2,3$.

The balanced lift is the alternating period-two phase and has edge $8$.  The
antipodal word has edge $\etaedge<8$ by Section~\ref{sec:period8}.  It remains
to rule out the three nonantipodal pairs.  This can be done by one exact
closed-walk recurrence rather than three spectral computations.  For a
starting residue $r$, let $f_\ell^{(r)}(j)$ be the signed walk sum of length
$\ell$ from $r$ to $j$ on the periodic lift, with
$f_0^{(r)}(j)=\delta_{rj}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 f_{\ell+1}^{(r)}(j)=f_\ell^{(r)}(j-1)+f_\ell^{(r)}(j+1)
 +\tau_{j-2}f_\ell^{(r)}(j-2)+\tau_jf_\ell^{(r)}(j+2).
 \label{eq:walk-recurrence}
\end{equation}
Let $M_k$ be the phase-averaged moment
\eqref{eq:phase-averaged-moments} and set
$E_k=M_{k+1}-8M_k$.  If the squared edge were at most $8$, then $E_k\leq0$
for every $k$.  For defect separations $1,2,3$, respectively, recurrence
\eqref{eq:walk-recurrence} gives the first positive excesses
\begin{equation}
 E_4=5504,\qquad E_6=64336,\qquad E_9=2872096.
 \label{eq:period8-excesses}
\end{equation}
All quantities are integers obtained by the displayed recurrence.  Thus each
nonantipodal word has edge above $8$.

\begin{theorem}[Period-eight trichotomy]
\label{thm:period8-trichotomy}
Among legal period-eight $Q$-words, modulo translation, reflection, and lift,
the antipodal two-defect class $Q_*$ is the unique class with squared Bloch
edge below $8$.  The balanced class has edge $8$, and every other class has
edge above $8$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The moment reduction leaves precisely the five classes described above.  The
balanced and antipodal values are exact, and
\eqref{eq:period8-excesses} excludes the other three.  Proposition
\ref{prop:invariances} justifies the orbit quotient and the choice of lift.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-period} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:period8-trichotomy} together prove
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-rigidity}.  Period eight is not merely the first cell
size at which one selected example works: the low-edge orbit at that first
period is rigid.

The proof suggests a broader question.  Is this rigidity only a consequence
of checking periods at most eight, or do the local defects of every low-edge
periodic word obey common restrictions?  The same moments answer the latter
question without attempting an all-period classification.
