# Journal of Graph Theory audit

## Scope and audit method

The journal explicitly welcomes structural graph results, theoretical graph algorithms, discrete optimization, and interactions between graph theory and other parts of mathematics.  The relevant editorial question is therefore not whether the paper contains a graph, but whether the graph-theoretic phenomenon remains visible after the matrix calculation is removed.

Official pages: [journal](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/10970118), [product information](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/10970118/homepage/productinformation.html), [author guidelines](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/10970118/homepage/forauthors.html).

The corpus below is a venue-calibration corpus, not the bibliography proposed for the final paper.  `F` means that theorem order and section architecture were inspected in accessible full text; `M` means that only publisher metadata and abstract-level theorem content were verified.  For `M` entries no claims are made about introduction length, proof length, or exact theorem placement.

Abbreviations in the architecture column:

- `E`: main theorem stated in the first 2–3 pages;
- `P`: dedicated preliminaries section;
- `R-int`: related work integrated into the introduction;
- `R-sec`: separate related-work section;
- `C-end`: conclusion or discussion section;
- `Comp`: material computation is part of the proof;
- `Fig`: nontrivial figures/tables are used.

## Structural findings from the full-text subset

### Introduction

The common order is short and functional:

1. define the graph problem and the exact invariant;
2. locate the closest prior theorem or conjecture;
3. state the main theorem early;
4. say in one paragraph what new mechanism proves it;
5. give a brief section map only when the paper is long.

The successful articles do not spend several pages advertising broad applications.  Related work is usually embedded around the precise problem, not placed in an encyclopedic standalone survey.

### Theorem package

JGT tolerates a special family when the result is complete within that family or the family exposes a graph-theoretic mechanism.  A counterexample can carry a paper when it settles a recognized statement and the construction has a transparent reusable principle.  A bare computation of one matrix family is a weaker fit.

For the present paper the theorem ladder should be:

1. **Exact family theorem:** for every (L\ge1), the positive-holonomy period-eight signing has squared spectral radius (4+\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}).
2. **Counterexample consequence:** for every (L\ge4), it strictly beats the twisted signing on (C_{8L}(1,2)).
3. **Minimal-period theorem:** no legal phase of period below eight has squared Bloch edge below eight.
4. **Period-eight rigidity:** at period eight, the antipodal two-defect class is the unique sub-eight class modulo the stated symmetries.
5. **General obstruction:** moment inequalities constrain defect density and clustering for arbitrary periodic words.

The first theorem is exact, the third explains “why eight,” and the fourth turns an explicit example into a graph-structural phase.  This is substantially stronger than leading with a rational separator.

### Preliminaries and proof flow

A short preliminary section is acceptable, but the corpus favors definitions close to their first theorem.  For this paper:

- introduce signed graphs, switching, (C_n(1,2)), and spectral radius together;
- prove the gauge-coordinate proposition immediately;
- introduce finite Bloch decomposition only after the period-eight word has been displayed;
- keep the (8\times8\to4\times4\to2\times2) algebra in one self-contained section;
- move no essential proof into an appendix.

### Computation and visuals

Computation is acceptable when the proof contract is explicit and finite.  The period-(<8) result uses nine symmetry representatives and exact integer certificates; this should be disclosed in a compact table, with the recurrence and certificate verification written in the text.  No search logs, code listings, or legacy enumeration belong in the article.

Useful visuals are limited to:

1. one signed (C_8(1,2)) cell showing the triangle-flux word and antipodal defects;
2. one dependency diagram from switching coordinates to the exact finite theorem;
3. optionally one exact dispersion plot as intuition, clearly not proof.

## Six venue questions

### 1. What level does the current package reach?

It reaches a plausible JGT research-article package, but not a high-confidence acceptance package solely as a counterexample.  The exact finite theorem plus the minimal-period theorem changes the evaluation: the article now contains an exact infinite family and a structural explanation of the first possible low-edge period.

### 2. Which strengthening most improves fit?

The minimal-period theorem.  The exact radius removes an avoidable weakness, but “period eight is first” supplies the graph-theoretic meaning that an editor can see before reading the matrix algebra.

### 3. Which graph objects must remain foregrounded?

The fixed underlying graph, switching classes, triangle-sign/flux word, Hamilton holonomy, periodic signing, defect positions, and natural dihedral/cell symmetries.

### 4. Which operator details should be compressed?

General magnetic-operator formalism, abstract direct-integral terminology, and speculative chiral classifications.  State only the finite direct sum actually used.

### 5. Is the period-eight trichotomy a sufficient second theorem?

Together with the new minimal-period theorem, yes.  Alone it is too local and could look like an eight-bit case check.

### 6. Should the moment obstruction remain?

Yes, but as a short final structural section.  It adds breadth and explains defect geometry.  It must be labeled necessary, not presented as a classification, and should not interrupt the exact proof.

## JGT-specific decision

JGT is a credible first submission if the manuscript is written as a graph-structure paper whose algebra solves the graph problem.  The article should be compact, theorem-forward, and about 22–28 journal pages.  The title should mention signed cycle squares, spectral radius, and period eight; it should not mention the 2026 preprint or “computer-assisted proof.”
