# Linear Algebra and its Applications audit

## Scope and audit method

LAA publishes new information and insight in matrix theory and finite-dimensional linear algebra, including algebraic, analytic, arithmetic, combinatorial, geometric, and numerical aspects, as well as substantial applications of matrix methods.  The period-eight paper fits only if the finite Hermitian matrices, exact eigenvalue reduction, or structural matrix mechanism are themselves prominent.

Publisher journal page: [Linear Algebra and its Applications](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/linear-algebra-and-its-applications).

As in the JGT audit, `F` denotes accessible full-text structural inspection and `M` denotes metadata/abstract verification only.  No uninspected theorem placement or proof length is inferred.

## Corpus-derived structural rules

### What LAA accepts as a complete unit

The full-text subset shows several recurring units:

- a clean matrix theorem followed by graph applications;
- an exact spectrum or characteristic polynomial for a structured family;
- a restricted but complete spectral characterization;
- a general block reduction with multiple nontrivial consequences;
- an exact extremal theorem for a matrix family defined by graphs.

The result need not solve a field-wide problem.  It must, however, deliver an exact or conceptually reusable matrix insight rather than only verify one numerical inequality.

### Placement and density

Main theorems normally appear in the introduction or at the start of the first substantive section.  Preliminaries can be longer than in JGT when several matrix/operator notions are essential.  Long algebraic derivations are acceptable in the body when each transformation exposes structure.  Raw computer algebra output is not a substitute for a derivation.

### Handling matrix algebra

The paper should show:

1. the explicit (8\times8) Hermitian fiber once;
2. the anticommuting involution and its eigenspace basis;
3. the off-diagonal (4\times4) block;
4. the determinant identity producing (P(y,c));
5. the shifted quartic and the exact root formula;
6. monotonicity in (c\in[-2,2]).

It should not display repeated determinant expansions, CAS transcripts, or all intermediate minors.  A short direct verification identity is preferable to “a symbolic computation gives.”

## Evaluation of the present theorem package

### Is the current (H(z))/chiral/quartic package enough?

Yes for a plausible LAA paper, because the strengthened result no longer ends at a bound.  It produces a closed dispersion law

\[
r(c)=4+\sqrt{8+c+\sqrt{26-3c}},
\]

proves strict monotonicity on ([-2,2]), and derives exact finite spectra at the upper edge for both holonomies.  The finite consequence is not a numerical postscript; it follows from exact phase quantization.

The caveat is novelty: chiral block reduction in itself is standard.  The new matrix content must be presented as the discovery and exact solution of this structured signed-circulant family, not as invention of the general off-diagonal principle.

### Value of the exact finite spectral radius

It is decisive.  Without it, the article looks like a certificate for a comparison inequality.  With it, the central theorem gives an exact algebraic spectral radius for every member of an infinite family:

\[
\rho(A_{8L,+})^2=4+\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}.
\]

The negative sector adds a second exact formula,

\[
\rho(A_{8L,-})^2
=4+\sqrt{8+2\cos(\pi/L)+\sqrt{26-6\cos(\pi/L)}}.
\]

This is a good secondary theorem because it demonstrates how holonomy changes the finite phase grid.

### Value of the now-proved general chiral criterion

The final strengthening round proves an iff in the explicitly delimited
monomial half-cell class.  This raises the LAA ceiling because the
`8 x 8 -> 4 x 4` reduction is now an instance of a gauge-invariant theorem,
not an isolated basis choice.  The manuscript must retain the limitation to
that class and must not imply a classification of arbitrary unitary chiral
involutions.

### How much graph background?

Keep enough to define switching, triangle fluxes, the fixed graph (C_n(1,2)), and the twisted comparison.  Move the broader signing-optimization history to two introduction paragraphs.  The body should turn quickly to the matrix family and finite Bloch decomposition.

## Recommended LAA theorem ladder

1. general monomial half-cell chiral iff and dimension-halving consequence;
2. finite Bloch decomposition for the displayed period-eight signed circulant;
3. period-eight chiral reduction and exact characteristic polynomial;
4. complete four-branch dispersion and positive-holonomy finite spectral radius;
5. exact negative-holonomy finite spectral radius;
6. comparison with the twisted family;
7. primitive-period result and period-eight uniqueness as a structural application;
8. moment obstruction, only if space permits.

## LAA-specific decision

LAA is at least as plausible as JGT and may be the safer fit because the strongest new results are exact matrix-spectral formulas.  A target length of roughly 20–26 journal pages is appropriate.  Compared with the JGT version, the LAA version should promote the negative-holonomy formula and the shifted quartic, while shortening the general defect-moment section.
