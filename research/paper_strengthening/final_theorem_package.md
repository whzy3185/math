## Theorem I — General half-period chiral mechanism

Let `p=2m` and let `tau` be a `2m`-periodic Hamilton-gauge word.  Define

```text
(A_tau x)_i
 =x_(i-1)+x_(i+1)+tau_(i-2)x_(i-2)+tau_i x_(i+2).
```

Within the natural monomial half-cell class generated by the alternating
diagonal operator `D` and half-period translation `T_m`, a chiral involution
exists if and only if

```text
tau_(i+m)=-tau_i  for every i.
```

Equivalently, for `Q_i=tau_i tau_(i+1)`,

```text
Q_(i+m)=Q_i,
prod_(j=0)^(m-1)Q_j=-1.
```

On the `z`-fiber the correct normalization is any unit scalar satisfying

```text
gamma_m(z)^2=(-1)^m z^(-1),
J_z=gamma_m(z) D T_m.
```

Then `J_z` is a self-adjoint unitary involution and

```text
J_z H_tau(z)=-H_tau(z)J_z.
```

Consequently the spectrum is symmetric about zero, the characteristic
polynomial is even, and the squared spectral problem reduces from dimension
`2m` to dimension `m`.

**Proof type:** analytic coefficient calculation and flux telescoping.

## Theorem II — Exact period-eight solvability

For

```text
tau_*=(1,1,-1,1,-1,-1,1,-1),
```

the half-cell criterion holds.  Its squared-fiber polynomial has the four
branches

```text
y_(sigma,tau)(c)
 =4+sigma sqrt(8+c+tau sqrt(26-3c)),
sigma,tau in {+1,-1},  -2<=c<=2.
```

The corresponding Hermitian spectrum is

```text
{+-sqrt(y_(sigma,tau)(c)): sigma,tau in {+1,-1}}.
```

All fiber eigenvalues are simple, the four squared bands are disjoint, and
the central gap is

```text
(-sqrt(4-sqrt(10+2sqrt(5))),
  sqrt(4-sqrt(10+2sqrt(5)))).
```

For every `L>=1`, finite phase quantization gives

```text
rho(A_(8L,+))^2
 =eta
 =4+sqrt(10+2sqrt(5)),
```

and

```text
rho(A_(8L,-))^2
 =4+sqrt(8+2cos(pi/L)+sqrt(26-6cos(pi/L))).
```

The negative sector is strictly below `eta` for finite `L` and converges to
it as `L` tends to infinity.

**Proof type:** finite Bloch decomposition, chiral block reduction, exact
quartic solution, and monotonicity.

## Theorem III — First occurrence

Eight is the smallest primitive period of a legal periodic Hamilton-gauge
signing whose squared Bloch edge is strictly below eight.

The proof consists of:

1. the `M1,M2,M3` defect inequalities;
2. legality and dihedral reduction for displayed periods below eight;
3. nine exact determinant/Rayleigh certificates;
4. zone-folding invariance under cell repetition;
5. the primitive period-eight target with edge `eta<8`.

The alternating primitive period-two phase and its repeated cells attain
squared edge exactly eight.

**Proof type:** analytic moment reduction plus a finite exact certificate set.

## Theorem IV — Rigidity at first occurrence

Among legal primitive period-eight phases, the antipodal two-defect class is
the unique class with squared Bloch edge below eight, modulo cyclic
translation, reflection, and the exact lift convention.  The balanced class
has edge eight; every other period-eight class has edge above eight.

Lift invariance and dihedral conjugacy now supply the full proof hygiene for
the orbit quotient.  The three non-antipodal two-defect cases use the already
closed short exact recurrence.

**Proof type:** analytic local identities and small finite-exact recurrence.

## Structural support — General moment obstruction

For an arbitrary legal `p`-periodic defect word,

```text
M1=4p,
M2=20p+16d,
M3=118p+168d+96a+48b.
```

Squared edge at most eight implies

```text
d<=3p/4,
40d+96a+48b<=42p.
```

These necessary inequalities explain why low-edge phases require sparse,
weakly clustered defects.  They are not a general classification.
