\section{Switching Coordinates and the Reference Phase}
\label{sec:switching-reference}

\subsection{Edge signings, switching, and Hamilton gauge}
\label{subsec:edge-signings-switching-hamilton-gauge}

Write the vertices of \(G_n=C_n(1,2)\) as \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\).  Let
\(a_i\) be the sign of the distance-one edge \(\{i,i+1\}\), and let \(b_i\)
be the sign of the distance-two edge \(\{i,i+2\}\), with all subscripts
read cyclically.  Switching by
\(\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z}\in\{\pm1\}^n\)
changes these signs to
\[
a_i'=\varepsilon_i a_i\varepsilon_{i+1},
\qquad
b_i'=\varepsilon_i b_i\varepsilon_{i+2}.
\]
At the matrix level this is the orthogonal conjugacy
\(A_{\sigma'}=D_\varepsilon A_\sigma D_\varepsilon\), where
\(D_\varepsilon=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_i)\).  Switching therefore
preserves the complete spectrum, not only the spectral radius.

\begin{lemma}[Hamilton gauge]\label{lem:hamilton-gauge}
Every signing of \(G_n\) is switching equivalent to one for which
\[
a_0=\cdots=a_{n-2}=1,\qquad a_{n-1}=\alpha,
\]
where
\[
\alpha=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}a_i\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
The residual sign \(\alpha\), the Hamilton-cycle holonomy, is invariant
under switching.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set \(\varepsilon_0=1\) and recursively choose
\(\varepsilon_{i+1}=\varepsilon_i a_i\) for \(0\leq i\leq n-2\).
Then \(a_i'=1\) along the spanning path \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\).  On the closing
edge,
\[
a_{n-1}'=\varepsilon_{n-1}a_{n-1}\varepsilon_0
=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}a_i.
\]
The product of the signs around a cycle is unchanged by switching because
every vertex factor occurs twice.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Flux words, holonomy, and the attaining candidate}
\label{subsec:flux-words-holonomy-attaining-candidate}

Before fixing a gauge, define the triangle flux at \(i\) by
\[
\tau_i=a_i a_{i+1}b_i.
\]
Each switching factor again occurs twice, so \(\tau_i\) is invariant.
In the Hamilton gauge of Lemma~\ref{lem:hamilton-gauge}, the actual
distance-two signs are
\[
b_i=\tau_i\quad(0\leq i\leq n-3),\qquad
b_{n-2}=\alpha\tau_{n-2},\qquad
b_{n-1}=\alpha\tau_{n-1}.
\]
Consequently \((\tau,\alpha)\) parametrizes the switching classes
bijectively once the vertex labels and seam are fixed.  It is often cleaner
to absorb the closing sign into the boundary condition.  More precisely, let
\[
\mathcal U_n^\alpha
=\{u:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C:u_{j+n}=\alpha u_j\}.
\]
On this \(n\)-dimensional space the signed adjacency takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-tau}
(A_\tau u)_i
=u_{i-1}+u_{i+1}+\tau_{i-2}u_{i-2}+\tau_i u_{i+2}.
\end{equation}
This twisted-boundary realization includes every edge that crosses the
Hamilton-gauge seam and is unitarily identical to the original finite
signed matrix.  We suppress \(\alpha\) from \(A_\tau\) when the boundary
space \(\mathcal U_n^\alpha\) is displayed explicitly.

The quadrilateral-flux word is
\[
Q_i=\tau_i\tau_{i+1}.
\]
It satisfies \(\prod_iQ_i=1\).  Conversely, a cyclic word
\(Q\in\{\pm1\}^n\) has a cyclic \(\tau\)-lift precisely when
\(\prod_iQ_i=1\); after choosing \(\tau_0\), the recurrence
\[
\tau_{i+1}=Q_i\tau_i
\]
determines that lift.  The two choices of \(\tau_0\) give \(\tau\) and
\(-\tau\), while \(\alpha\) remains independent.  Thus the exact cyclic
data are \((\tau,\alpha)\), or equivalently
\((Q,\tau_0,\alpha)\).  The reduced pair \((Q,\alpha)\) represents two
switching classes, a distinction that disappears only after passing to the
squared spectrum or spectral radius.  The lift condition
\(\prod_iQ_i=1\) and the Hamilton twist \(\alpha\) are independent.  If
\(n\) is even and
\((Du)_i=(-1)^iu_i\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lift-negation}
A_{-\tau}=-DA_\tau D,
\qquad
H_{-\tau}=DH_\tau D,
\qquad H_\tau:=A_\tau^2.
\end{equation}
The unsquared spectra of the two lifts are reflected through zero; their
squared spectra and spectral radii agree.

\begin{proposition}[Attainment by the twisted candidate]
\label{prop:candidate-attainment}
For every even \(n\geq8\), the class
\[
Q_i=-1\quad(0\leq i<n),\qquad \alpha=-1,
\]
has a representative \(\tau_i=(-1)^i\) satisfying
\[
\rho(A_\tau)^2
=4\cos^2\frac{\pi}{n}+4\cos^2\frac{2\pi}{n}
=4+2\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}+2\cos\frac{4\pi}{n}
=\theta_n.
\]
Consequently \(m_n\leq\rho_-(n)\) for every even \(n\geq8\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because \(\alpha=-1\), the allowed Fourier parameters on
\(\mathcal U_n^{-1}\) are
\[
\vartheta_k=\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{n},\qquad 0\leq k<n.
\]
Multiplication by \((-1)^j\) carries the mode \(e^{ij\vartheta}\) to
\(e^{ij(\vartheta+\pi)}\).  Hence the span of these two modes is invariant,
and in the displayed order the restriction of \(A_\tau\) is
\[
B(\vartheta)
=2\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\vartheta&\cos2\vartheta\\
\cos2\vartheta&-\cos\vartheta
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus
\[
B(\vartheta)^2
=4\bigl(\cos^2\vartheta+\cos^22\vartheta\bigr)I_2.
\]
With \(x=\cos2\vartheta\), the quantity in parentheses is
\[
F(x)=x^2+\frac{x}{2}+\frac12.
\]
The relevant values lie in
\([-1,\cos(2\pi/n)]\).  Since \(F\) is convex, its maximum is at an endpoint.
Now \(F(-1)=1\), whereas \(n\geq8\) gives
\[
F\left(\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)
\geq F\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)
=1+\frac1{2\sqrt2}>1.
\]
The maximum therefore occurs at
\(\vartheta=\pi/n\) or \(\pi-\pi/n\), which yields the first equality.
The double-angle identity yields the second.  Since this explicit signing is
in the minimization domain, the final inequality follows.
\end{proof}

The candidate in Proposition~\ref{prop:candidate-attainment} is the signing
that attains the conjectured comparison value.  It is not the period-eight
reference phase introduced in Subsection~\ref{subsec:period-eight-reference-phase}.
The latter is a different low-spectral bulk used to construct phase slips.

\subsection{The squared local operator}
\label{subsec:squared-local-operator}

For a bilateral word \(\tau\), equation~\eqref{eq:A-tau} defines a bounded
self-adjoint range-two operator on \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\).  Its square
\(H_\tau=A_\tau^2\) is the nonnegative range-four operator
\begin{align}
(H_\tau u)_i={}&4u_i+u_{i-2}+u_{i+2}\notag\\
&+\tau_{i-4}\tau_{i-2}u_{i-4}
+(\tau_{i-3}+\tau_{i-2})u_{i-3}\notag\\
&+(\tau_{i-2}+\tau_{i-1})u_{i-1}
+(\tau_{i-1}+\tau_i)u_{i+1}\notag\\
&+(\tau_i+\tau_{i+1})u_{i+3}
+\tau_i\tau_{i+2}u_{i+4}.
\label{eq:H-range-four}
\end{align}
Indeed, this follows by inserting equation~\eqref{eq:A-tau} four times into
\(A_\tau(A_\tau u)\) and collecting equal displacements.  The extreme
coefficients may also be written
\[
\tau_{i-4}\tau_{i-2}=Q_{i-4}Q_{i-3},
\qquad
\tau_i\tau_{i+2}=Q_iQ_{i+1},
\]
while, for example,
\(\tau_i+\tau_{i+1}=\tau_i(1+Q_i)\).  Thus a negative \(Q_i\) cancels the
associated odd-displacement coupling.  Formula~\eqref{eq:H-range-four} is
local and precedes every periodic or interface specialization.

Translation of the coefficient word is implemented by the bilateral shift.
Reflection reverses the word and is unitary after the corresponding index
change.  Equation~\eqref{eq:lift-negation} handles the two lifts.  We will
use only these explicit equivalences in the body; the fiber-level
zone-folding identities are recorded with the Floquet algebra in
Appendix~\ref{app:g6-certification}.

\subsection{The period-eight reference phase}
\label{subsec:period-eight-reference-phase}

The canonical reference word and its quadrilateral flux are
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}=(+,+,-,+,-,-,+,-),
\qquad
Q_{\mathrm{ref}}=(+,-,-,-,+,-,-,-),
\]
extended periodically.  The positive \(Q_{\mathrm{ref}}\)-sites are exactly
\(4\mathbb Z\).  More generally, for \(s\in\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\), let
\(B_s\) denote the translate whose positive \(Q\)-sites are
\(s+4\mathbb Z\).  Each \(B_s\) has two \(\tau\)-lifts; since one four-site
cell has \(Q\)-product \(-1\), those lifts are antiperiodic over four sites
and periodic over eight.

For a nonzero Bloch multiplier \(z\), impose
\[
u_{8m+r}=z^m v_r,\qquad 0\leq r<8,
\]
and write \(A_{\mathrm{ref}}(z)\) for the resulting unsquared \(8\times8\)
fiber.  It is Hermitian when \(|z|=1\).

\begin{proposition}[Reference squared edge]\label{prop:reference-edge}
Let
\[
\eta=4+\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}.
\]
Then
\[
\sup_{|z|=1}\rho\bigl(A_{\mathrm{ref}}(z)\bigr)^2=\eta<8,
\]
and equality occurs only at \(z=1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The exact determinant identity is derived and independently certified in
Appendix~\ref{app:g6-certification}.  We retain here the two algebraic steps
that locate the edge.  At \(z=1\), put \(y=x^2\) and \(X=y-4\).  The fiber
equation reduces to
\[
X^4-20X^2+80=0.
\]
Its largest \(y\)-root is
\(4+\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}=\eta\).

For global maximality, set
\[
s=\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5},\qquad u=y-\eta,\qquad
t=2-(z+z^{-1}).
\]
On the unit circle, \(t\in[0,4]\).  The same exact determinant relation,
after substitution, is
\begin{align*}
0={}&u^4+4su^3+2u^2t+(40+12\sqrt5)u^2
+4sut+8\sqrt5\,su\\
&+t^2+(4\sqrt5-3)t.
\end{align*}
Every displayed coefficient is positive.  If a squared fiber eigenvalue
satisfied \(y\geq\eta\), then \(u,t\geq0\), so the right-hand side could
vanish only for \(u=t=0\).  Thus \(y\leq\eta\), and equality forces
\(z+z^{-1}=2\), hence \(z=1\).  Finally,
\(\sqrt5<9/4\) and
\(\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}<191/50\) give
\(\eta<391/50<8\).
\end{proof}

The reference phase is also the zero-interface member of the gap language.
If consecutive positive \(Q\)-sites are separated by \(g=4\), the right
positive lattice is the continuation of the left lattice in the same
translate \(B_s\).  Up to translation, reflection, and the lift choice,
this is precisely the unperturbed reference bulk, not an abnormal interface.
