\subsection{Checkpoint and evidence boundary}

This supplement is assembled against the repository checkpoint
\begin{center}
\texttt{e365e1553ad73a8a534fb67f5ee76562521609ce}.
\end{center}
Every path listed below exists at that checkpoint.  The JSON certificates
store exact finite objects and rational enclosures.  The checker chain
re-evaluates their exact mathematical contracts.  Two coordinate
implementations separately audit the global unsquared exclusions, while
some symbolic transfer and elimination routines are shared and disclosed;
the package therefore does not claim a clean-room implementation of every
symbolic identity.  A successful checker is evidence only in
combination with the analytic reductions printed in the article and in this
supplement; it is not a replacement for those reductions.

No immutable external archive identifier has yet been assigned.  The
archive record and its persistent identifier are therefore pending and are
not represented by a provisional or invented DOI.

The exact-\(2r\) certificate binds the rank-two one-interface input, the
Floquet contraction and conditioning bounds, the Gram estimate, the
codimension-\(2r\) complement, the exact count, and the
\(2r\)-dimensional Feshbach constants.  Its focused suite contains tamper
tests for dimension, scope, constants, dependencies, and the coordinate
form of the Schur complement.

The single-gap structure certificate records transfer, Evans, quotient
symmetry, and recurrence data.  The Task 56 checker independently
reconstructs every integer vector and its full \(A_gv\) image.  The
uniform-separation certificate stores the threshold \eqref{eq:T}, all seven
quotient comparisons, and the minimum exact margin.  Its checker reconstructs
the rational comparisons and rejects non-strict or altered rows.  The
mathematical reason that seven
rows suffice is the finite-propagation argument in
\cref{sec:single-gap}, not an empirical scan over gap lengths.

\subsection{Scope of the supplement}

The separated-interface theorem is restricted to
\(r\in\{1,2,3\}\) and \(D\geq1040\).  It counts eigenvalues with
multiplicity and does not prove individual simplicity or a sign for the
leading interaction.  The single-gap theorem concerns positive single gaps
only.  Neither theorem classifies arbitrary multi-interface minimizers.
