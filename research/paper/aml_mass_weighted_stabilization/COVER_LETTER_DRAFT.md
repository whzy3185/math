Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Mass-weighted damping and a rate dichotomy for chemotaxis with signal-dependent motility,”** for publication in *Applied Mathematics Letters*.

We study the signal-dependent-motility system

\[
u_t=\Delta(\varphi(v)u),\qquad v_t=\Delta v+uF(v),
\]

and identify a quantitative stabilization mechanism based on positive conserved cell mass together with finite $L^2$ control of the cell density. An elementary mass-weighted coercivity estimate closes the signal energy without requiring an eventual pointwise lower bound for the cell density.

The resulting framework gives a rate dichotomy for general dissipative kinetics: quadratic damping yields exponential relaxation, while degenerate damping of order $2+\theta$ yields the algebraic signal rate $t^{-1/\theta}$. Under an eventual finite-$L^p$ bound with $p>\max\{n,2\}$, these rates propagate to full $L^\infty\times W^{1,\infty}$ stabilization. Spatially homogeneous solutions show that the signal exponent $1/\theta$ is optimal.

The manuscript is positioned explicitly against the existing large-time theory for direct-consumption signal-dependent-motility systems. Its contribution is the unified treatment of general dissipative kinetics, nonzero equilibria, degenerate damping, and the finite-$L^p$ transfer to strong-norm rates. As a timely application, the boundedness theorem of Qin and Zheng for the recent production--consumption model supplies the upstream hypothesis, while our argument identifies the homogeneous equilibrium and exponential relaxation rate. The superlinear-consumption example illustrates the degenerate branch under the regular-motility assumptions of our theorem.

The manuscript is concise, proof-oriented, and fits the six-page Letter limit in journal format. It has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. The authors declare no competing interests, and this research received no external funding.
