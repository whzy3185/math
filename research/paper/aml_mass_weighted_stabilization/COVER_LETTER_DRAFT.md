Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Mass-weighted damping and a rate dichotomy for chemotaxis with signal-dependent motility,”** for publication in *Applied Mathematics Letters*.

We study the system

\[
u_t=\Delta(\varphi(v)u),\qquad v_t=\Delta v+uF(v),
\]

under homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions. The paper identifies a quantitative stabilization mechanism based on positive conserved cell mass. The key estimate is a nonlinear mass-weighted coercivity inequality which converts the weighted signal dissipation into control of the full signal energy without requiring an eventual pointwise lower bound for the cell density.

This mechanism yields a rate dichotomy. Uniformly quadratic dissipativity of the signal kinetics produces exponential stabilization, while degenerate damping of order `2+theta` produces the signal rate `t^{-1/theta}` and an explicit range of algebraic rates for the full system. A further point is that eventual boundedness of the cell density in one finite space `L^p`, with `p>max{n,2}`, is sufficient for the uniform stabilization conclusion; an a priori `L^infinity` bound is not required by the abstract theorem.

The result applies directly to the production--consumption model recently studied by Qin and Zheng in *Applied Mathematics Letters* (2026). Their boundedness theorem provides the upstream boundedness hypothesis, whereas the present argument identifies the limiting homogeneous state and its exponential relaxation. The framework also includes superlinear signal consumption and displays the transition from exponential to algebraic decay. A spatially homogeneous family gives the exact `t^{-1/theta}` signal profile in the degenerate case.

We have positioned the manuscript against the direct signal-consumption results of Li and Zhao and the simultaneous production--consumption stabilization result of Tao and Winkler. Accordingly, the manuscript does not claim that stabilization for signal-dependent motility or for production--consumption kinetics is new in itself. The contribution is the combined mass-weighted damping principle, its explicit rate dichotomy, the weakening to eventual finite-`L^p` control, and its application to the recent production--consumption model.

The manuscript is concise and proof-oriented and is intended to fit the Letter format. It has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal.
