# Narrative methods for three graph theory manuscripts

This study supports revision of the domination, Mycielski, and signed-circulant manuscripts. Its conclusion is editorial: a paper becomes easier to evaluate when the question, theorem hierarchy, mechanism, witnesses, and limitations are explicit. Better narrative does not make a theorem new or correct.

## Scope and sampling

The operational sample is seven English-language mathematical papers with a verified China-based institutional affiliation on the studied version. The date window is 5 October 2023 through 5 October 2026. This is an affiliation-based purposive sample, not a claim about nationality, Chinese-language writing, or a distinctive national style. Six author teams are represented, with overlapping Nankai connections and one repeated Xinjiang team. International collaborations are included. Selection favors relevance to exact bounds, structural stability, fractional parameters, and signed spectra, plus open full text and inspectable theorem/proof architecture. No bibliometric ranking or representativeness claim is made.

Full PDFs were obtained. Reading covered every abstract and introduction, the theorem and section map, substantial proof passages, and available closing discussions. Exact reading coverage and inspected figure pages are in source_catalog.json. This is a narrative study, not an independent verification of every proof. It does not authenticate every priority claim in the sources or constitute a novelty search for our manuscripts.

## How to use the study

1. Read the seven short, page-specific notes under reading_notes, together with source_catalog.md
2. Apply writing_rubric.md as a revision checklist, without using its scores as a novelty assessment
3. Use application_plan.md to revise the current manuscripts in editable copies
4. Require a statement-preservation check and an independent mathematical audit after editing

## Main editorial decisions

- Start from the precise mathematical uncertainty the present paper resolves
- Put inherited results, new theorems, conditional consequences, and finite computations in distinct roles
- Explain the mechanism before technical detail; a list of section numbers is insufficient
- Attach optimality to a quantity, parameter regime, and explicit witness
- Give examples and figures a proof-related job
- Admit exactly what a theorem or method does not establish
- Transfer these structural choices in original wording; do not copy sentence templates or prestige language
